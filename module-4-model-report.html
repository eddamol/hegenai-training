Model report: case study report

Invented for Module 4 of the HEGenAI training. The organisation, data and the Jensen & Holm source are made up. This page is public so that a feedback agent can use it as knowledge.

How this example is meant to be used: It shows what work at the higher levels of the rubric can look like. It is one example, not a template. Students should not copy its wording, its organisation or its sources. A feedback agent may quote at most one sentence of it to show what a level looks like.

Where this report sits on the rubric

It is a strong report, not a perfect one. In a test run of a feedback agent with the Module 4 rubric, it was placed as follows.

  • Problem analysis: Meets. It states a research question and analyses the problem with two course concepts, Porter’s five forces and Herzberg’s two-factor theory, supported by data about the café. To reach Exceeds, it would compare what the two concepts reveal, for example external competition against internal staff turnover.
  • Recommendation: Developing. It explains how the actions address the problem, but only the stamp card is backed by evidence. The advice on schedules and pay has none, so it has fewer than two pieces of evidence for the recommendation as a whole.
  • Writing and structure: Meets. All six required sections, with headings, a research question in the introduction, and APA 7 in-text citations and references.

The report (shortened to about 450 words)

Introduction

This report asks: why do fewer customers return to Café Nord, a 40-seat café in central Aarhus, and what should it do about it? Its sales fell 18% from 2024 to 2025. The report gives background on the café, analyses the problem and recommends a solution.

Background

This section describes Café Nord as it is today. It opened in 2015, is run by its owner, and has eight staff on part-time contracts with weekly changing schedules. Most of its sales come from regular customers who live or work nearby.

Problem analysis

This section analyses why customers do not return. Since 2024, the share of customers who return within a month has fallen from 46% to 31% (Café Nord sales data, 2025). Two course concepts explain this. First, in Porter’s five forces, the threat of substitutes has grown: two chain cafés opened within 300 m in 2024, both with lower prices. Second, in Herzberg’s two-factor theory, weak hygiene factors such as pay and irregular schedules explain why five of eight staff left in 2024 (owner interview). New staff do not know the regular customers, and in a survey of 40 customers, 29 named “staff who know me” as their main reason for coming.

Recommendation

This section recommends how Café Nord can win back regular customers. It should first offer fixed schedules and match local pay, because staff turnover is the cause the analysis found behind the loss of personal service. It should then add a paper stamp card: in a study of 30 small Danish cafés, stamp cards raised return visits by 10–15% (Jensen & Holm, 2022), at about DKK 2,000 a year against DKK 40,000 for an app.

Conclusion

Fewer customers return to Café Nord because cheaper competitors arrived and staff turnover removed the personal service its regulars came for. Stable staff, followed by a stamp card, is the recommended response.

References

Herzberg, F. (1966). Work and the nature of man. World Publishing.

Jensen, M., & Holm, L. (2022). Stamp cards and repeat visits in small Danish cafés. Nordic Journal of Retail Studies, 9(2), 45–58.

Porter, M. E. (2008). The five competitive forces that shape strategy. Harvard Business Review, 86(1), 78–93.
